Google Ads Location Targeting for Small Business: Presence vs Interest (2026)

If your business serves one city, one county or a handful of ZIP codes, every click from outside that area is money spent on someone who cannot buy from you. Location targeting is the setting that decides who sees your ads, and the default option in Google Ads is often not the one a local business wants.

This guide explains how Google Ads location targeting works, the difference between presence and presence or interest, when to use radius targeting, how to exclude areas, and how to check the report that shows where your clicks really came from.

What location targeting controls

Location targeting is set at the campaign level. It tells Google which geographic areas your ads may show in and, just as important, how Google decides whether a person counts as being "in" that area. You can target:

  • Countries, states and regions - useful for online stores that ship nationwide.
  • Cities, counties, metro areas and ZIP codes - the usual choice for service businesses.
  • A radius around an address or point - good for a single shop, clinic or restaurant.

You can also add excluded locations, so your ads never show to people in those places even if they fall inside a wider target.

Presence vs presence or interest: the setting that matters most

Under Location options in your campaign settings, Google offers two targeting choices:

  • Presence or interest: people in, regularly in, or who have shown interest in your targeted locations. This is the default.
  • Presence: people in or regularly in your targeted locations.

The difference is the word interest. With the default, someone in another state who searches for "plumber Dallas" or browses content about Dallas can see an ad from a Dallas plumber. That makes sense for a hotel, a wedding venue or a tourist attraction, where people plan from far away. It rarely makes sense for a plumber, a dentist, a gym or a roofing company, who can only serve people who are physically nearby.

Which option should a small business choose?

  • Local service business (trades, home services, clinics, salons, gyms): choose Presence.
  • Destination business (hotels, venues, attractions, relocation services): Presence or interest can be the right choice, because buyers research before they arrive.
  • Online store shipping to set countries: Presence in the countries you ship to, so you do not pay for clicks from places you cannot deliver to.

There is a matching setting for exclusions. Keep it on Presence: people in your excluded locations so exclusions block the people actually located there.

Radius targeting vs city and ZIP targeting

Radius targeting draws a circle around an address. It is quick to set up and works well when customers come to you, for example a café or a physiotherapy clinic. It has two weaknesses: a circle ignores rivers, highways and city lines, and it can include areas you do not serve.

City, county and ZIP code targeting follows real boundaries. It is usually better when you travel to the customer, because your service area is defined by where your vans go, not by a perfect circle. Many small businesses use a mix: a tight radius around the shop plus the specific ZIP codes where most past customers live.

Whichever you pick, start smaller than you think. It is easier to expand a profitable area than to find which part of a large area is wasting budget.

Exclusions: stop paying for clicks you cannot convert

Exclusions are the fastest win in most small accounts. Common cases:

  • A city you target sits next to a neighborhood or town you do not serve. Exclude it.
  • Your state target includes regions too far to travel to. Exclude those counties.
  • You ship only to the US and Canada. Make sure no other country is targeted, and that the targeting option is Presence.

Pair location exclusions with search-term exclusions. If people search for your service plus a city you do not cover, add that city name as a negative keyword so the ad does not match the query in the first place.

Check where your clicks really came from

Settings show your intent; the locations report shows what happened. In Google Ads, open Insights and reports, then When and where ads showed, then Locations. Switch the view to see where people were located when they saw your ads, and compare it with your targeted areas.

Look for three things:

  1. Clicks from outside your service area. If you see them, check that the targeting option is Presence and add exclusions.
  2. Areas with spend but no conversions. After a fair amount of data, these are candidates for exclusion or a lower bid.
  3. Areas that convert well. These may deserve their own campaign with a separate budget, so they are not starved by weaker areas.

A regular PPC account audit should include this report, because location waste builds up quietly as campaigns are copied and edited over time.

Location bid adjustments and Smart Bidding

With manual CPC or enhanced bidding you can raise or lower bids by location, for example +20% inside your core ZIP codes and -30% at the edge of your area. With Smart Bidding strategies such as Maximize Conversions or Target CPA, Google sets the bid for each auction itself and ignores location bid adjustments, except a -100% adjustment, which works like an exclusion. If you use Smart Bidding and want to treat areas differently, split them into separate campaigns with their own budgets or targets instead.

A 10-minute location setup checklist

  1. List the cities, counties or ZIP codes you actually serve, or the address and distance customers travel.
  2. Add them as targeted locations in each campaign.
  3. Set Location options to Presence (unless you are a destination business).
  4. Keep exclusions on Presence and add the areas you do not serve.
  5. Add negative keywords for city names outside your area.
  6. After two to four weeks, open the Locations report and remove areas that spend without converting.
  7. If one area clearly performs best, give it its own campaign and budget.

Frequently asked questions

What is the default location targeting option in Google Ads?

The default is "Presence or interest", which includes people who are in your targeted locations, regularly in them, or have shown interest in them. Most local service businesses should change it to "Presence".

Why do I get clicks from outside my target area?

The most common reason is the "Presence or interest" option, which lets people elsewhere see your ads if they show interest in your area. Location signals are also not perfect, so a small share of out-of-area clicks can happen even with "Presence". Check the Locations report and add exclusions where needed.

Is radius targeting better than ZIP code targeting?

Radius targeting is simpler and suits businesses customers visit. ZIP code or city targeting follows real boundaries and suits businesses that travel to customers. Many small businesses combine both.

Do location bid adjustments work with Smart Bidding?

No. Smart Bidding strategies set bids per auction and ignore location bid adjustments, except a -100% adjustment. To treat locations differently under Smart Bidding, use separate campaigns.

How often should I review location performance?

Check the Locations report two to four weeks after a change, then at least monthly as part of your regular account review.

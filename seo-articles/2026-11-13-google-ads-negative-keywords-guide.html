Why negative keywords matter for a small budget

Every Google Ads search campaign shows ads for searches that are close to your keywords, not only for the exact words you typed in. That is useful, because it finds customers you did not think of. It also means part of your budget can go to searches that will never turn into a sale: job seekers, people looking for free versions, students researching a topic, or buyers who want a product you do not sell. Negative keywords tell Google which searches should not show your ad. For a small business spending a few thousand dollars a month, they are one of the quickest ways to move money from wasted clicks to useful ones.

How negative keyword match types work

Negative keywords have three match types, and they behave differently from normal keywords. The most important difference: negative keywords do not automatically block close variants such as plurals, misspellings or synonyms. If you add free as a negative, you may still need freeware separately.

Negative broad match

Your ad will not show if the search contains all of your negative words, in any order. A negative broad keyword cheap shoes blocks "shoes that are cheap" but not "cheap boots".

Negative phrase match

Your ad will not show if the search contains the exact phrase in the same order, even with other words around it. A negative phrase "used cars" blocks "used cars near me" but not "cars that are used".

Negative exact match

Your ad will not show only when the search is exactly that phrase with no extra words. Use this when a short query is wrong for you but longer versions are valuable.

Where to find negative keywords

You do not need to guess. Google Ads shows the real searches that triggered your ads in the search terms report (Insights and reports, then Search terms). Sort by cost, then read the top rows and ask one question for each: would this person ever buy from us?

  • Job and career searches: "jobs", "salary", "hiring", "internship", "resume".
  • Free and DIY intent: "free", "template", "how to make", "diy", "pdf".
  • Research and education: "definition", "what is", "course", "wikipedia", "examples" (keep these if you sell training).
  • Wrong product or service: brands you do not carry, services you do not offer, locations you do not serve.
  • Competitor names: only if you have decided not to bid on them on purpose.

If you run a free PPC audit of your account, wasted search terms are usually one of the first findings, because they are easy to measure: cost with no conversions.

Campaign, ad group or account level?

Where you add a negative keyword decides how much traffic it blocks.

  • Ad group level: use it to steer searches to the right ad group. For example, block "emergency" in your standard plumbing ad group so those searches go to the emergency ad group with the right ad and landing page.
  • Campaign level: use it for terms that are wrong for one campaign but fine for another.
  • Shared negative keyword lists: build a list once (for example "Jobs and careers") and apply it to many campaigns. New campaigns get protection in one click.
  • Account-level negatives: for terms that are never right for the business, such as adult or unrelated brand terms. These also apply to Performance Max campaigns, which give you fewer search-term controls than standard search campaigns.

A weekly 20-minute routine

  1. Open the search terms report for the last 7 days and sort by cost.
  2. Review every term that spent money without a conversion.
  3. Add clear mismatches as negatives at the right level, choosing the narrowest match type that still blocks the problem.
  4. Add good, converting terms as normal keywords so you control their bids.
  5. Once a month, check your negative lists for conflicts: a negative that blocks one of your own keywords will quietly stop that keyword from showing. Google Ads flags many of these in the Recommendations tab.

Example: a local plumbing company

Imagine a plumbing company bidding on phrase match keywords such as "water heater repair" and "emergency plumber". After two weeks the search terms report shows clicks from "plumber salary texas", "how to fix water heater pilot light yourself", "water heater repair course" and "plumbing supply store near me". None of these people is looking to hire a plumber today. The owner adds a shared list called "Jobs and DIY" with salary, jobs, course, yourself and diy as negative broad keywords, and adds "supply store" as a negative phrase at campaign level. The next week the report is shorter and the same budget reaches more homeowners who need a repair. The exact savings depend on the account; the point is that every one of those removed clicks was money spent on someone who was never going to call.

Performance Max and Shopping campaigns

Performance Max and Shopping campaigns do not use keywords in the same way as search campaigns, so negative keywords play a smaller but still useful role there. Account-level negatives apply to them, which makes the account-level list the right place for terms that are never relevant to your business. For Shopping, product titles and descriptions in your feed decide much of the matching, so clean, accurate product data is as important as negatives.

Common mistakes

  • Blocking too broadly. A negative broad keyword like cheap may block buyers searching "cheap but reliable plumber". Review a negative against your own keyword list before saving.
  • Forgetting close variants. Add singular and plural, and common misspellings, as separate negatives where they matter.
  • Adding negatives once and never again. Search behaviour changes with seasons and news. A weekly look keeps waste low.
  • Not tracking conversions. Without conversion tracking you cannot tell a wasted search term from a good one. Fix tracking first.

Starter negative keyword list

Every business is different, so treat this as a starting point to review, not a list to paste blindly: jobs, job, careers, hiring, salary, internship, free, template, diy, pdf, course, training, definition, wikipedia, reddit. Remove anything that matches what you sell; a training company should keep "course", for example.

Frequently asked questions

How many negative keywords should a small campaign have?

There is no ideal number. Add negatives for the irrelevant searches you actually see in your search terms report. A new campaign often starts with a short shared list and grows week by week.

Do negative keywords block close variants?

No. Unlike normal keywords, negative keywords do not automatically include plurals, misspellings or synonyms, so add important variants separately.

Can negative keywords hurt performance?

Yes, if they are too broad. A negative that overlaps with one of your own keywords can stop good searches from showing your ads. Check for conflicts monthly.

How often should I review search terms?

Weekly for active campaigns, and after any large change such as new keywords, a new match type or a budget increase.

Google Ads Auction Insights: How to Run a Competitive Paid Search Audit (2026)

When your Google Ads costs climb or your impressions drop and nothing in your account has changed, the cause is often outside your account: a competitor raised bids, launched a new campaign or started bidding on your brand name. Google gives you one report that shows this directly, Auction Insights. Used well, it turns guesswork into a short, repeatable competitive paid search audit.

This guide walks small-business advertisers through what each Auction Insights metric means, how to read the report without over-reacting, and which changes actually win back impression share.

What a competitive paid search audit answers

A normal PPC audit looks inward: account structure, keywords, ads, landing pages and conversion tracking. A competitive audit looks outward and answers four questions:

  • Who am I really competing with? The businesses that show up in the same auctions are not always the ones you think of as rivals.
  • How often do I show compared with them? If a competitor appears in twice as many auctions as you do, they are taking clicks you could have had.
  • Who wins the top positions? Ads at the top of the page get most of the clicks on many searches.
  • Is the gap caused by budget or by Ad Rank? The fix is completely different for each.

If you have not done an inward audit recently, start there first. Our free PPC audit guide covers the account-level checks; the competitive audit below is the layer on top.

Where to find Auction Insights in Google Ads

Open Google Ads, select Campaigns, then choose Insights and reports and Auction insights. You can run the report for the whole account, a single campaign, an ad group or individual keywords. It is available for Search and Shopping campaigns, and Google also shows auction data for Performance Max where enough traffic exists.

Two practical notes:

  • Pick a long enough date range. Thirty days is a sensible minimum for a small account. With very little traffic the report may be empty, because Google only shows it when there is enough auction activity.
  • Segment by time. Use the segment option to split the data by week or month. A single snapshot tells you who is there; a trend tells you who just arrived.

The six Auction Insights metrics, in plain English

Impression share

The percentage of auctions you were eligible for in which your ad actually showed. In the report, each competitor's row shows their impression share in the same auctions. If you sit at 35% and a competitor at 70%, they appear twice as often as you on the searches you both target.

Overlap rate

How often a competitor's ad appeared in the same auction as yours, when yours showed. A high overlap rate means you are fighting for exactly the same searches.

Position above rate

When you both showed, how often the competitor's ad was placed above yours. A rising position above rate is an early warning that someone is outbidding you or improving their ad quality.

Top of page rate

How often an ad appeared anywhere above the organic results. Compare your own figure with each competitor's.

Absolute top of page rate

How often an ad was the very first ad on the page. This position usually has the highest click-through rate, and usually the highest cost.

Outranking share

How often your ad ranked higher than a given competitor's, or showed when theirs did not. It is the closest thing to a head-to-head score in the report.

A 30-minute competitive audit, step by step

Step 1: Run the report at campaign level

Start with your most important campaign, usually the one that spends the most or produces the most leads. Note the top five domains by impression share.

Step 2: Add your own lost impression share columns

In the campaign view, add the columns Search lost IS (budget) and Search lost IS (rank). These tell you why you missed auctions:

  • Lost to budget means your daily budget ran out. You were good enough to show, but you could not afford every auction.
  • Lost to rank means your Ad Rank was too low. Ad Rank combines your bid, the expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience.

Step 3: Compare this month with last month

Segment by month and look for changes: a new domain in the list, a competitor whose impression share jumped, or a rise in position above rate. Change is more useful than any single number.

Step 4: Run it on your brand keywords separately

Filter to your brand campaign or brand keywords. If other companies show up with a meaningful overlap rate, they are bidding on your name. That raises your brand cost per click and can send customers who searched for you to someone else.

Step 5: Drill into your top non-brand keywords

Run the report at keyword level for your five highest-spending non-brand keywords. Competition is rarely the same across all of them; you may be winning on some and losing badly on others.

Step 6: Write down one action per finding

Do not leave the audit with a list of observations. Every finding should end with a decision: change a budget, improve an ad, adjust a bid, or deliberately do nothing.

How to act on what you find

If you are losing impression share to budget

You are already competitive on quality; you are simply running out of money early in the day. Options: move budget from weaker campaigns, narrow the campaign to your best-converting keywords and locations, or reduce spend on hours and days that rarely convert. Adding budget only makes sense if your cost per conversion on this campaign is acceptable.

If you are losing impression share to rank

Raising bids is the expensive fix. Work on the cheaper levers first:

  • Tighten ad groups so each ad matches the search more closely.
  • Rewrite headlines to include the main keyword and a clear reason to choose you.
  • Use every relevant asset: sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets and, for local businesses, location.
  • Improve landing page speed and make sure the page delivers exactly what the ad promised.

If a competitor bids on your brand

Keep a brand campaign running so your own ad holds the top position; brand clicks are usually cheap because your relevance is high. If a competitor uses your trademark in their ad text, Google has a trademark complaint process you can use. Bidding on your brand name as a keyword is generally allowed, so focus on outranking them rather than stopping them.

If a new competitor appears with very high impression share

Do not start a bidding war on day one. New entrants often spend aggressively for a few weeks and then pull back. Watch your cost per conversion, not your position. If profitability holds, let them pay for the top spot.

Common mistakes in competitive PPC analysis

  • Chasing position for its own sake. Being first is only worth it if the extra clicks convert at a cost you can afford.
  • Reading one week of data. Small accounts have noisy data; use 30 days or more and look at the trend.
  • Ignoring the cause. A competitor's higher impression share might come from a much bigger budget, not better ads. You cannot fix that by changing headlines.
  • Forgetting your own changes. If you lowered bids or paused ads, your numbers moved for reasons that have nothing to do with competitors. Check your change history first.

Making the audit a monthly habit

A competitive audit is most useful when it is repeated. Put a 30-minute block in your calendar each month, export the Auction Insights report for your main campaigns, and keep the results in one sheet so you can see trends over a quarter.

Frequently asked questions

What is a competitive paid search audit?

It is a review of how your search ads perform against the other advertisers in the same auctions: how often you show compared with them, who takes the top positions, and whether you lose auctions because of budget or Ad Rank. In Google Ads, the main source for it is the Auction Insights report.

How often should I check Auction Insights?

Once a month is enough for most small businesses, plus an extra check whenever your cost per click or impressions change suddenly without a change on your side.

Why is my Auction Insights report empty?

Google only shows the report when there is enough auction activity for the selected campaign, keyword and date range. Try a longer date range or run it at campaign level instead of keyword level.

Can I see how much competitors bid?

No. Auction Insights shows how often competitors appear and where they rank relative to you, not their bids, budgets or conversion data.

Is it a problem if a competitor bids on my brand name?

It raises your brand costs and can divert some customers, but bidding on another company's brand as a keyword is generally allowed in Google Ads. The best response is a strong brand campaign. Use of your trademark inside their ad text is a separate matter that Google's trademark policy covers.

Should I always try to reach the absolute top position?

No. Top positions cost more. Aim for the position that gives you the lowest cost per conversion, which for many small businesses is not first place.
